# SYSTEM QUALITY

1. Reliability: Availability, Consistency, Resiliency

2. Observability: The ability to know WHAT IS HAPPENING in your system.

3. Security: The ability to safeguard your system and its data.

4. Scalability: The ability to handle increase or decrease in system usage.

5. Adaptability: The ability to HANDLE CHANGING requirements or user behaviors

6. Performance

- Latency: How QUICKLY does the system respond.

- Throughput: How MUCH data can move through the system at a given time.

# Chesterton's fence and how does it relate to system security

Chesterton's fence is a concept illustrated by a story of a person who sees a gate in the middle of a road and opens it, not realizing there's a dangerous bull on the other side.

The principle suggests that before removing or dismissing existing safeguards, you should understand why they were put in place.

In system security, this means assuming that previous developers and security measures were implemented for good reasons, such as why banks have strict regulatory compliance, logging, and auditing requirements.

# Designing systems to scale both up and down

is important because of cost efficiency and resource management.
Running systems at maximum capacity at all times is wasteful and expensive.
Many applications experience seasonality, where traffic varies by time of year (like e-commerce during Black Friday versus January).
Cloud computing enables systems to scale up during high-traffic periods and scale down during quieter times, optimizing resource usage and costs.
